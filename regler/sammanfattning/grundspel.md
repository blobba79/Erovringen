# Base game (Carcassonne, The Basic Game) - rules summary

Source: Complete Annotated Rules v7.3, s. 10-26 and the tile list s. 224-273. The River and The Abbot are NOT covered here.

## Components
- 72 land tiles, one of them with a dark reverse (the start tile). Tiles show road, city and field segments, plus cloisters and crossings. (s. 10)
- 48 followers in six colors: 8 per color. One per color is the scoring marker. A follower can act as knight, monk, thief or farmer. (s. 10, 11)
- One scoreboard (a 50-field track that is lapped). (s. 10, 17)
- Point tiles ("50" / "100" on the reverse) mark laps. (s. 17)
- 2 to 6 players. (s. 10)

## Setup
- Place the start tile in the middle of the table. Shuffle the other tiles and put them face down in stacks (or in a bag). (s. 11)
- Each player takes a color, puts one follower on field 0 of the scoreboard and keeps 7 in the supply. (s. 11)
- The youngest player decides who starts. (s. 11)
- The edge of the table limits the play area, and the whole layout may in principle not be shifted. (s. 24) This is a table rule, not a game rule. Our digital version decides this itself.

## Turn (clockwise, in this order) (s. 11)
1. Draw and place a tile (mandatory).
2. Optionally deploy one follower on the tile just placed.
3. Score features completed by the tile.

### 1. Place a tile (s. 11-12)
- The tile is drawn and shown to the other players.
- At least one edge must touch a tile already in play. Corner-to-corner contact does not count as a connection. (s. 11, 11 fn 7)
- All edges must fit the neighbours (road to road, city to city, field to field). One matching side is not enough. (s. 12 fn 9)
- Cloisters may lie next to each other or corner to corner. A cloister does not need eight neighbours to be placed. A cloister is never "connected" to; it sits in the middle of a field segment. (s. 12 fn 8)
- If a tile cannot be placed anywhere legal and all players agree, it is removed from the game and a new one is drawn. (s. 12)
- Roads always continue to another road segment on every edge; a road cannot end in nothing. (s. 14 fn 16)

### 2. Deploy a follower (s. 12-13)
- At most one follower per turn, from the player's own supply, and only to the tile just placed. (s. 12)
- The role is chosen by the place: thief on a road, knight in a city, monk on a cloister, farmer on a field segment. Farmers lie down. (s. 12)
- Not allowed if the road, city or farm that the segment belongs to already holds any follower, including the player's own, however far away. Check the whole connected feature. (s. 13)
- A tile with several segments: the player may choose which one. A farmer may also be placed on the field around a cloister; the cloister then stays unoccupied. (s. 12 fn 13)
- Completing an unoccupied city does not require placing a knight there. (s. 12 fn 12)
- With no followers left the player only places tiles. (s. 13)
- Deploy happens before scoring. A follower deployed on a feature completed this turn is scored and returned at once, but cannot be redeployed this turn. Example: a tile with two city segments completes a small city; if the player already owns that city, he or she may deploy to the other segment; if not, he or she chooses one segment. (s. 13 fn 14)

### 3. Score completed features (s. 14-16)
Order when a follower is deployed on a feature completed this turn: complete with tile, deploy, score, return the follower. (s. 16)
Features count as completed as soon as the tile is placed (important for The Flier). (s. 16 fn 22)

- **Completed road**: both ends are a crossing, a city segment or a cloister, or the road forms a closed loop. Score 1 point per tile the road covers. (s. 14)
  - Every crossing or junction ends a road, whichever way the road reaches it. Thieves do not enter the village at the junction. (s. 14 fn 15, 17)
- **Completed city**: no gaps in the city and fully enclosed by walls. Score 2 points per city segment and 2 extra per pennant. (s. 14, 15)
  - Two segments of the same city on one tile count as one segment. (s. 15)
  - A pennant only affects the segment it is on, not the whole tile. (s. 15 fn 20)
  - The old "small city" rule (2 tiles = 2 points) is not used; a two-segment city scores normally, 4 points. (s. 15 fn 19)
- **Completed cloister**: surrounded by 8 tiles. The monk scores 9 points (1 per tile, including the cloister tile). (s. 16)
- **Majority**: with several thieves or knights, the player with the most followers scores the full value. A tie means all tied players score the full value. (s. 15)
  - Two followers of one player do not double the points. The number of followers only decides the majority. (s. 15 fn 21)
- **Return**: after scoring, thieves, knights and monks go back to their owner's supply and can be used from the next turn. Not before scoring. (s. 16)
- **Farmers** are never returned during the game (but see the note on expansions, s. 16 fn 24). (s. 16)

## Farms (s. 16-17)
- Connected field segments form a farm. Farms give no points during the game. (s. 16)
- Farms are separated by roads, cities (if the city does not lie within the farm) and the edge of the play area. (s. 17, 21) River segments also separate farms. (s. 17 fn 25) River tiles belong to another summary.
- A tile can join farms that already hold farmers, so farmers from several players can end up in one farm. Then the player placing the tile may not add a farmer to it. (s. 17)
- Farms may be huge and may stay open all game. (s. 16 fn 23)

## Scoreboard (s. 17)
- Score is recorded immediately. When a marker reaches or passes field 0, the player shows a point tile with "50" face up; the next lap turns it to "100"; a third lap gives another tile with "50" showing. (s. 17)

## End of the game and final scoring (s. 18-20)
- The game ends at the end of the turn in which the last land tile is placed. Features completed on that turn are scored normally first. (s. 18)
- Final scoring, in this order:
  1. Incomplete roads, cities and cloisters. 1 point per segment/tile, and pennants count only 1 point each. The followers are then removed. (s. 18)
     - A road gives 1 point per tile, regardless of how many thieves. (s. 18 fn 29)
     - An incomplete cloister gives 1 point for itself plus 1 per neighbouring tile (5 neighbours = 6). (s. 18 fn 29)
     - Majority rules are the same as in play. Example: GREEN with the most knights in a big incomplete city scores 8 and BLACK scores nothing. (s. 18)
  2. Farms. The owner is the player with the most farmers on the farm. A tie means all tied players are owners. Each owner scores 3 points per completed city that borders the farm or lies within it. (s. 18-19)
     - A city bordering several farms gives 3 points to the owner(s) of each farm. (s. 19)
     - Incomplete cities give nothing. Incomplete farms are scored normally. (s. 19 fn 30)
     - A bordering city must have a wall edge against the farm; touching at a single corner is not enough. (s. 20 fn 34)
     - A farm closed in by roads with no adjacent city scores 0. (s. 19 fn 31)
     - This is the "third edition" method. (s. 19 fn 32)
- The player with the most points wins. A tie is not handled by the HiG rules; the other publishers say shared victory (RGG) or play again (ZMG). (s. 20 fn 35)
- OKLART: whether a Carcassonne Abbey tile may still be played after the last tile is covered by the Abbot summary (s. 18 fn 28), not by this one.

## Interaction with expansions
- Farmers and the point tiles also appear in expansions (e.g. Inns and Cathedrals ones). Base-game scoring is the baseline. (s. 16 fn 24, s. 17)
- Several expansions change when a follower may be returned (Festival, Dragon). (s. 16 fn 24)
- Inns and Cathedrals: a road with an inn scores 0 at the end of the game. (s. 18 fn 29)
- Size of followers (Inns and Cathedrals) does not change points, only majority. (s. 15 fn 21)
