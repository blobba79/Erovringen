# The Gold Mines

The Gold Mines put gold bars on the tiles. Whoever wins a road, city or cloister with gold on it takes the bars, and at the end of the game collected gold is worth points.

## What is included
- 8 tiles with a gold symbol.
- 16 gold bars. They lie on the tiles and belong to no one until someone takes them.

## How a turn works
- When you place a gold tile, place it as usual. Then you put one gold bar on that tile and one on an adjacent tile, diagonals included.
- A tile can receive more gold even if it already has some.
- Then you may deploy a follower as usual.
- Gold belongs to the whole tile, not to one feature on it.

## Gold is awarded
- When a road, city or cloister is completed, the player with the majority there takes all gold bars lying on the tiles that are part of it. A cloister (and the abbot's garden) counts its own tile and all eight neighbours.
- With a tied majority, or when several features with gold are completed at once, gold is awarded **one bar at a time**. The player whose turn it is gets the first, then it goes clockwise until all is awarded. The game picks which bar each player gets.
- When starting the game you can choose the other variant, where gold is awarded per completed feature instead.
- Farmers get no gold.

## Scoring at the end of the game
Gold left on tiles is worth nothing. The gold you have collected scores like this:
- 1 to 3 bars: 1 point per bar
- 4 to 6 bars: 2 points per bar
- 7 to 9 bars: 3 points per bar
- 10 or more: 4 points per bar

Seven bars are worth 21 points.

## Good to know
- Gold cannot be eaten by the dragon or captured.
- The game handles the awarding and totals your bars.
